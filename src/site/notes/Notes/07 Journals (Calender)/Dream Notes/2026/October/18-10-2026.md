# Dream Note — Schools and Tuitions Reopen in the Middle of Durga Puja

> [!abstract] Dream Summary
> On 18 October, I had a dream in which all schools and tuition centres unexpectedly reopened in the middle of Durga Puja. At a time when students were supposed to be enjoying the festive holidays, spending time with their families, visiting pandals, and relaxing at home, the entire academic routine suddenly returned. The dream created an unusual contrast between the festive atmosphere of Durga Puja and the demands of school and tuition.

## 1. Basic Dream Information

- **Date of dream:** 18 October 2026
- **Dream title:** Schools and Tuitions Open in the Middle of Durga Puja
- **Dream category:** School, Education, Daily Life, Festive Season
- **Dream type:** Realistic and situational dream
- **Main setting:** Schools, tuition centres, homes, and the surrounding festive environment
- **Main event:** Schools and tuition centres reopen unexpectedly during the Durga Puja holidays.
- **Central theme:** The sudden interruption of holidays by academic responsibilities.

## 2. The Dream Narrative

### 2.1. An Ordinary Durga Puja Holiday

The dream began during the Durga Puja season, when the usual academic routine was supposed to be suspended. The atmosphere was associated with the festive break, when schools remained closed and students could temporarily forget about their regular classes, homework, examinations, and tuition schedules.

Durga Puja was supposed to be a period of relaxation, celebration, and freedom from the usual responsibilities of school. The sudden return of academic activities was therefore completely unexpected.

Everything seemed normal at first. The holidays were still underway, and there was no apparent reason to believe that the usual routine would return so soon.

However, the situation was about to change dramatically.

### 2.2. The Unexpected Announcement

Suddenly, I discovered that schools were reopening in the middle of Durga Puja.

The announcement was particularly surprising because the holidays were not supposed to have ended yet. Instead of waiting until the festive break was over, schools were apparently resuming their regular operations while the celebrations were still taking place.

The idea itself seemed absurd. Durga Puja is one of the most significant festivals in West Bengal, and the school holidays surrounding it are an important part of the annual academic calendar.

Yet, in the dream, this familiar arrangement was disrupted.

It was as though the academic calendar had suddenly been changed, with normal school days inserted into the middle of the festival.

### 2.3. Every School Opens

The situation was not limited to one school.

In the dream, **all schools appeared to reopen during Durga Puja**. The reopening was widespread rather than an isolated decision by a particular institution.

This made the situation even more unusual. If only one school had reopened, it might have seemed like a local administrative decision. But when every school followed the same schedule, it felt as though the entire education system had collectively decided to interrupt the holidays.

Students who had expected to remain at home suddenly had to think about attending classes again.

School uniforms, school bags, books, notebooks, timetables, and classroom routines became relevant once more. The familiar responsibilities that had temporarily disappeared during the holidays returned to everyday life.

The dream transformed a period traditionally associated with freedom from school into a period in which school attendance once again became an obligation.

### 2.4. Tuition Centres Also Reopen

The situation became even more inconvenient when tuition centres also reopened.

It was not enough for schools to resume their activities. Tuition classes, which usually add another layer of academic responsibility to a student's schedule, also returned during the festive break.

This meant that students could potentially have to manage both regular school classes and private tuition while Durga Puja was still underway.

The holidays no longer felt like a proper break.

Instead, the normal academic routine seemed to have returned from every direction. Even the time that might otherwise have been available for relaxation, hobbies, entertainment, or family activities was threatened by the possibility of attending classes.

The combined reopening of schools and tuition centres made the dream feel particularly frustrating and unrealistic.

### 2.5. The Contrast Between Education and Celebration

The most striking feature of the dream was the contrast between the two environments.

On one side was Durga Puja, with its festive atmosphere, celebrations, pandal visits, decorations, cultural programmes, food, family gatherings, and time away from regular schoolwork.

On the other side was the academic routine, involving attendance, lessons, assignments, examinations, tuition, and the pressure to keep up with studies.

Normally, the festive holidays create a temporary separation between these two parts of life. Students can devote more time to celebrations without having to follow their ordinary school schedules.

In the dream, however, that separation disappeared.

Education suddenly occupied the same period that was supposed to be reserved for the festival. The result was an unusual situation in which a major cultural celebration and the ordinary academic routine existed simultaneously.

The dream presented a world in which the calendar still said Durga Puja, but the institutions responsible for education were behaving as though the holidays had already ended.

## 3. The Most Unusual Aspects of the Dream

Several elements made this dream different from an ordinary dream about attending school.

### 3.1. The Timing

The reopening happened in the middle of Durga Puja rather than at the end of the holidays.

The timing was the central source of the dream's absurdity. The problem was not simply that school had reopened, but that it had reopened at a moment when students would ordinarily expect to remain on holiday.

### 3.2. The Scale of the Reopening

The dream involved all schools and tuition centres rather than a single institution.

This gave the event a system-wide quality. It was as though the entire academic system had adopted an unexpected schedule at the same time.

### 3.3. The Disappearance of the Holiday

The dream effectively removed the distinction between a school day and a holiday.

Even though Durga Puja was still underway, its usual effect on the academic calendar had disappeared. The festive period continued to exist, but the freedom normally associated with it was reduced.

### 3.4. The Return of Multiple Responsibilities

The reopening of both schools and tuition centres meant that academic responsibilities returned together.

Rather than having one additional activity, students were suddenly expected to resume the broader routine that normally occupies their time during the school term.

### 3.5. The Unrealistic Nature of the Situation

The entire situation felt unusual because it contradicted the expected organisation of the festive season.

Dreams can combine familiar institutions, events, and concerns in ways that would be unlikely in real life. In this case, the familiar concept of school reopening was placed in an unexpected context.

## 4. Possible Interpretation of the Dream

> [!note] Important
> Dream interpretations are possibilities, not established facts. A dream does not necessarily have a hidden meaning, and its content cannot reliably predict future events.

### 4.1. The Desire for a Genuine Holiday

One possible interpretation is that the dream reflects the importance of holidays as a break from everyday responsibilities.

Festivals such as Durga Puja offer an opportunity to step away from routine, enjoy personal interests, and spend time on activities that are not directly connected to academics.

The sudden reopening of schools and tuition centres could represent the opposite of this freedom: the feeling that ordinary obligations can return even when a break is expected.

### 4.2. Academic Routine Entering Personal Time

The dream may also illustrate the contrast between structured academic time and personal time.

School and tuition follow timetables, whereas holidays allow a more flexible schedule. In the dream, the structured academic routine entered a period that was supposed to remain relatively free.

This could simply reflect how familiar school-related thoughts and routines remain, even when school is not in session.

### 4.3. An Unexpected Change in Plans

Another possible interpretation concerns unpredictability.

The dream introduced a major change without giving students the time to prepare for it. A situation that was expected to remain stable suddenly changed, forcing everyone to reconsider their plans.

This pattern can appear in dreams involving school, travel, appointments, examinations, and other familiar aspects of life.

### 4.4. The Conflict Between Obligation and Enjoyment

The dream can also be understood as a fictional conflict between what one is expected to do and what one would prefer to do.

Academic responsibilities represent obligation, while the festive holiday represents an opportunity for enjoyment and personal choice.

The dream placed both in direct competition with one another.

However, this does not necessarily mean that the dream expresses a deep psychological conflict. It may simply be a humorous or exaggerated combination of familiar experiences.

## 5. Possible Emotions Associated With the Dream

Depending on how the dream actually felt, several emotional reactions could have been involved.

- **Surprise:** Schools reopening during the festival was unexpected.
- **Confusion:** It was unclear why the usual holiday schedule had changed.
- **Frustration:** The reopening of schools and tuition centres could interfere with the break.
- **Disbelief:** The idea of every educational institution reopening simultaneously was unusual.
- **Disappointment:** A holiday that was supposed to provide free time could become another period of academic work.
- **Amusement:** The situation was so contrary to normal expectations that it could also be interpreted as a strange or funny dream scenario.

These are possible reactions rather than confirmed emotions. The actual emotional tone of the dream depends on what I experienced while dreaming.

## 6. Connection With Everyday Life

School is a recurring part of a student's daily life. Even when classes are suspended, familiar routines such as studying, attending tuition, preparing for examinations, and following academic schedules remain part of the broader experience of education.

Durga Puja, by contrast, creates a temporary change in the normal routine.

The dream combined these two familiar parts of life and rearranged their usual timing. Instead of allowing the festive period to interrupt the academic routine, it made the academic routine interrupt the festive period.

This reversal is what made the scenario distinctive.

The dream did not require an unfamiliar setting or an extraordinary event. Its unusual quality came entirely from changing the timing of something familiar.

The following interpretations are speculative and should not be treated as universal meanings.

| School reopening | The return of academic responsibilities |

The most important element is the timing of the reopening. The same event would have felt ordinary if it had happened after the holidays. Placing it in the middle of Durga Puja transformed it into the central absurdity of the dream.

## 8. Reality Versus Dream

It is useful to distinguish the imagined situation from the real-world academic calendar.

- **Dream scenario:** Schools and tuition centres reopen in the middle of Durga Puja.
- **Expected holiday arrangement:** Schools generally observe a scheduled festive break according to their academic calendar.
- **Dream's central disruption:** The normal holiday period is interrupted unexpectedly.
- **Real-world conclusion:** The dream itself provides no evidence that schools or tuition centres will actually reopen during the festival.

The dream should therefore be recorded as a personal dream experience rather than interpreted as a report of an actual policy change.

## 9. Final Reflection

The dream of 18 October 2026 presented a simple but striking situation: all schools and tuition centres reopened in the middle of Durga Puja.

What made the dream memorable was not the reopening of schools by itself, but the fact that the reopening happened at the wrong time. The festive holiday was supposed to offer a temporary break from the academic routine, yet the dream removed that separation completely.

Schools returned, tuition centres returned, and the ordinary expectations of academic life suddenly occupied a period that was supposed to be different.

The dream can be viewed as an example of how familiar experiences are rearranged in unexpected ways during sleep. It combined the ordinary structure of education with the extraordinary timing of a holiday interruption.

Ultimately, it was a dream about an unexpected reversal of expectations: **a festival holiday that suddenly stopped behaving like a holiday.**
