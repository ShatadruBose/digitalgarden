**Dream Note**

Last night the dream came soft and wet and close.

It was my birthday. The room was warm, the light low and golden, and my friends gathered around me like a living circle. Someone held a thick slice of cake—dense, creamy, the frosting already softening from the heat of their hands—and guided it slowly to my open mouth. Fingers brushed my lips as they pressed the cake between them. I felt the cool sweetness hit my tongue, then the deliberate slide of fingertips along my teeth, tracing the edges, lingering over every ridge and groove.  

They didn’t stop there. One by one they slipped a finger past my lips and rubbed the surface of my teeth, slow and thorough, as if mapping something private. The cake mashed against enamel and tongue; saliva welled up and mixed with the frosting until everything was slick and shared. I could feel the gentle pressure of their fingertips polishing my teeth, the soft scrape, the intimate knowledge of every surface inside my mouth. My own tongue moved without thinking, rubbing against their fingers, coating them, tasting the faint salt of their skin beneath the sweetness.

When the cake was half-eaten—soft, broken, thoroughly soaked in my saliva—they took it back without hesitation. They brought the same piece to their own mouths and ate what I had already claimed, licking the frosting that still carried the warmth and wetness of me. No flinch, no distance. Just quiet acceptance, as if sharing the inside of my mouth was the most natural thing in the world. Some of them licked their fingers clean afterward, the ones that had been inside me, tasting the mixture of cake and spit and the faint metallic edge of teeth.

The dream stayed that close the whole time: the pressure of fingers against enamel, the slow wet exchange, the unhesitating way they swallowed what had been in my mouth. It felt less like a party and more like a private ritual of closeness—bodies and mouths and sweetness folding into one another until the boundaries blurred.

I woke with the taste of frosting still ghosting on my tongue and the memory of their fingers still pressed against my teeth.
