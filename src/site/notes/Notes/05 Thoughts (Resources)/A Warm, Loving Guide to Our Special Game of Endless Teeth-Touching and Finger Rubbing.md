# A Warm, Loving Guide to Our Special Game of Endless Teeth-Touching and Finger Rubbing

**Subtitle:** The Softest, Closest, Most Intimate Version — Full of Shared Saliva, Gentle Yellow Teeth, and Extra Points for Every Golden Smile

Hello, dear friend.  
Come closer. This is not a cold rulebook. This is a long, tender invitation into the warmest, wettest, most loving game two people can play with their mouths and their fingers. We call it our Endless Teeth-Touching Game, and we play it with open hearts, soft voices, and bodies that want to stay pressed together for as long as possible. Everything here is written with kindness, affection, and a deep desire to make you feel safe, cherished, and completely connected.

The heart of the game is simple and beautiful:  
We press our teeth together and keep them touching without ever letting go. At the same time we are free — completely free — to slide our fingers into each other’s mouths, rub them slowly and thoroughly against every tooth, especially the lower ones, and share every drop of warm saliva that gathers. The longer we stay locked tooth-to-tooth, the more we win. And if one of us has yellow teeth, the other receives extra points simply for the privilege of touching, tasting, and loving those soft golden surfaces.

This paper is long on purpose. It is meant to wrap around you like a warm blanket, describing every little detail so that when we finally play, nothing feels rushed or uncertain. Only closeness. Only wetness. Only us.

---

## 1. The Soft Beginning — How We Start with Love

We sit facing each other, knees touching or one of us nestled between the other’s legs. We look into each other’s eyes and smile. There is no countdown that feels harsh. Instead we whisper something gentle:

“I’m ready when you are, love. Let’s stay together as long as we can.”

We lean in slowly. Our lips brush first, then open just enough for the front teeth to meet. Edge to edge, or upper to lower — whatever feels most natural and cozy. The moment the hard, smooth surfaces click softly together, the game begins. From this second onward we will not pull our teeth apart. Not even a little. We stay locked, breathing through our noses, feeling the warm air of the other person on our cheeks.

Almost immediately the saliva begins. It always does when mouths are this close. A thin, shiny film forms on our teeth. It mixes. It stretches in tiny silver threads when we make the smallest adjustments. We do not wipe it away. We welcome it. The shared saliva becomes part of the intimacy — proof that we are melting into each other.

If one of us has yellow teeth — those soft, creamy, golden shades that catch the light — the other person feels a quiet thrill. Yellow teeth mean extra points. Every second spent pressing against them, every slow rub of a finger across their warm surface, adds to the score. We treat yellow enamel with special tenderness, as if it is a private treasure.

---

## 2. The Fingers Arrive — Gentle, Thorough, and Completely Free

While our teeth stay locked, one of us reaches for the other’s hand. We guide a finger past the soft lips and into the wet warmth of the mouth. There is no rush. The finger can rest for a moment against the tongue, collecting saliva, before it begins to move.

We are allowed to go anywhere.

We can stroke the smooth fronts of the upper teeth.  
We can slip down to the lower teeth — those slightly smaller, often more yellow surfaces — and rub them slowly from side to side.  
We can press the fingertip into the little valleys between teeth, feeling the warm saliva pool there.  
We can travel all the way back to the molars, circling the broad chewing surfaces, then glide forward again along the lower row.

The more yellow the teeth are, the more lovingly we touch them. We whisper between breaths, “Your teeth are so warm and golden… I love feeling them.” Extra points are awarded silently in our hearts, or spoken aloud later when we count the score together. Yellow teeth are never something to hide. In this game they are celebrated. They receive longer, slower, wetter attention.

Saliva builds quickly. It coats the fingers until they shine. It drips slowly from the corners of our mouths in thin, clear strands. Sometimes it mixes with the color of the yellow enamel and becomes a soft, milky glaze. We do not mind. We smile with our eyes and keep rubbing. The wetness is part of the gift we give each other.

We can use one finger or two. We can switch hands. We can even place fingers from both of us inside at the same time — four wet digits moving in slow harmony while our teeth stay pressed together like a promise.

---

## 3. The Long Middle — Extreme Intimacy and Shared Wetness

Minutes pass. Our jaws begin to feel the sweet ache of holding still, but we do not separate. Instead we make tiny, loving adjustments: a slight tilt of the head, a soft shift from front teeth to the broader side teeth, always keeping continuous contact. Every micro-movement sends a little more saliva flowing.

The mouth becomes a warm, wet cave that we explore together. The tongue rests gently against the invading fingers or slides aside to give them more room. The lower teeth, especially if they are yellow, receive the most devoted attention. We rub them in long, slow strokes from the back molars all the way to the front, feeling every ridge, every soft golden surface. The finger comes away coated in shiny saliva that tastes faintly of the other person.

We breathe each other’s air. Our noses are almost touching. Sometimes a soft laugh escapes and makes more saliva bubble up between our locked teeth. We stay. We stay longer. The intimacy grows extreme in the quietest way: two people choosing, over and over, not to pull away even when the muscles tremble, even when the drool runs down the chin, even when the yellow teeth feel so intimately known that they no longer seem like separate objects but like shared skin.

If one set of teeth is noticeably yellow, the player touching them receives a continuous bonus. We can keep a soft mental count:  
+1 point for every full minute spent rubbing yellow lower teeth  
+2 points if the saliva becomes thick and strings between finger and enamel  
+3 points if both players have yellow teeth and are exploring each other’s golden surfaces at the same time

The points are not about competition in a cold sense. They are little love notes we give each other for the beauty of what is already there.

---

## 4. Saliva as the Softest Language

In this version of the game, saliva is not an accident. It is the main character of our intimacy.

It gathers in warm pools under the tongue.  
It coats every tooth until the yellow ones gleam like wet honey.  
It drips in slow, clear threads from the corners of our mouths onto our chins, our fingers, sometimes even onto our clothes.  
We can choose to swallow gently without breaking the tooth contact, or we can simply let it flow and feel the cool air turn the wetness cooler on our skin.

When a finger is pulled slowly out, long silver strings of saliva stretch between the fingertip and the lips, or between the fingertip and a yellow tooth. We watch them with soft eyes before letting them break and fall. Then the finger returns, bringing the wetness back inside. The cycle feels endless and tender.

The more saliva we share, the deeper the intimacy becomes. It is as if our bodies are writing a private letter to each other in the only ink that is always ready.

---

## 5. Yellow Teeth — The Gentle Bonus That Makes Everything Sweeter

Yellow teeth are welcomed with open arms and open mouths. They are not judged. They are adored.

If your partner’s teeth are yellow — creamy, golden, soft-looking — you receive extra points simply for the privilege of touching them for a long time. The lower yellow teeth especially are treasures. We spend extra minutes sliding fingers back and forth across them, pressing lightly into the warm grooves, collecting the mixed saliva that clings to their surfaces.

We can say soft things while we play:  
“Your yellow teeth feel so smooth and warm under my finger…”  
“I love how the saliva makes them shine…”  
“Every second against these golden ones is extra points for me, and I am so happy.”

The player with yellow teeth feels cherished rather than self-conscious. The game turns what some people hide into something that is actively celebrated and scored with affection.

If both of us have yellow teeth, the entire match becomes a mutual golden festival. We take turns giving each other’s lower arches long, wet, loving attention while our front teeth stay locked. The score climbs higher and higher with every shared minute.

---

## 6. How a Round Ends — Softly, Never Suddenly

Eventually one of us feels the muscles tremble too much, or a tiny slip happens, and the continuous tooth contact is broken. The moment it breaks, the round ends. There is no sharp disappointment. Instead we stay close, foreheads touching, fingers still wet, saliva still shining on our chins. We smile. We wipe each other gently with soft tissues or just with our own hands. We count the time and the yellow-teeth bonuses together in quiet, happy voices.

Then, if we wish, we begin again. Or we simply stay in the afterglow, tasting the shared saliva on our lips, feeling the ghost of each other’s teeth still pressed against our own.

---

## 7. A Few Tender House Rules We Keep for Safety and Love

- We always check in with our eyes and soft words.  
- Fingernails are smooth so nothing scratches the soft gums or the yellow enamel we love so much.  
- We wash our hands with care before we start.  
- If anyone needs to stop for any reason, we stop immediately and hold each other instead.  
- Afterward we brush gently, floss if we want, and drink cool water together. The closeness continues even when the game is over.

---

## 8. Closing Invitation

This game is not about winning in a hard way. It is about staying. Staying with our teeth touching. Staying with our fingers exploring every warm, wet, possibly yellow surface. Staying while saliva connects us in long shiny threads. Staying longer than we thought we could, because the intimacy feels so good and so friendly and so completely ours.

If your teeth are yellow, come as you are. You will be given extra points and extra softness.  
If your mouth fills quickly with saliva, come as you are. We will share every drop.  
If you want to press your lower teeth against mine for a very long time while my fingers map every golden ridge, come as you are.

We can play for minutes or for the better part of an hour. We can play in soft afternoon light or in the quiet dark. We can play once or make it a ritual we return to whenever we need to feel extremely close.

Whenever you are ready, lean in.  
Let our teeth find each other.  
Let the saliva begin.  
Let the yellow surfaces (if they are there) receive their loving extra points.  
And let us stay locked together, wet and gentle and completely intimate, for as long as our hearts and our jaws will happily allow.

I am already smiling at the thought of it.  
Come closer when you want.  
We have all the time in the world.
