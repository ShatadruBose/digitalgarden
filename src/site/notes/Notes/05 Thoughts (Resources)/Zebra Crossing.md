# Zebra Crossing

## Introduction

A **zebra crossing** is a designated place on a road where pedestrians can safely cross. It is marked with a series of broad white parallel stripes painted across the road, resembling the black-and-white stripes of a zebra, which is how it gets its name. Zebra crossings are one of the most common road safety features found in towns and cities around the world.

---

## Purpose of a Zebra Crossing

The main purpose of a zebra crossing is to:

- Provide a safe place for pedestrians to cross the road.
- Alert drivers that people may be crossing.
- Reduce road accidents involving pedestrians.
- Improve traffic discipline and road safety.
- Encourage walking by making roads safer.

---

## Features

A typical zebra crossing has the following features:

- Broad white stripes painted across the road.
- Warning signs placed on both sides.
- Often located near schools, hospitals, markets, parks, and busy intersections.
- May be equipped with flashing lights or beacons in some countries.
- Usually placed where pedestrians frequently cross.

---

## Rules for Pedestrians

When using a zebra crossing, pedestrians should:

1. Stop at the edge of the road.
2. Look right, left, and right again (or according to the traffic direction in their country).
3. Ensure vehicles have stopped or are slowing down.
4. Walk across the crossing without running.
5. Stay alert and avoid using mobile phones while crossing.

---

## Rules for Drivers

Drivers should:

- Slow down when approaching a zebra crossing.
- Stop to allow pedestrians to cross.
- Never overtake another vehicle near a zebra crossing.
- Watch carefully for children, elderly people, and people with disabilities.
- Resume driving only after the crossing is completely clear.

---

## Importance

Zebra crossings are important because they:

- Save lives.
- Prevent accidents.
- Make roads safer for everyone.
- Help children cross roads safely.
- Support people with disabilities and senior citizens.
- Promote responsible road behavior.

---

## Advantages

- Easy to recognize.
- Inexpensive to install.
- Improves pedestrian safety.
- Organizes traffic movement.
- Reduces confusion at busy roads.
- Encourages drivers to remain cautious.

---

## Disadvantages

- Some drivers ignore the rules.
- Faded road markings become difficult to see.
- Poor lighting at night may reduce visibility.
- Heavy traffic may still create risks if drivers fail to stop.

---

## Safety Tips

- Always use the nearest zebra crossing whenever available.
- Do not assume every vehicle will stop.
- Make eye contact with drivers before crossing.
- Cross only in a straight line.
- Keep children close while crossing.
- Avoid distractions such as texting or listening to loud music.

---

## Zebra Crossings Around the World

Different countries have slightly different rules:

- In many countries, vehicles must stop whenever a pedestrian is waiting at a zebra crossing.
- Some countries add flashing amber lights for better visibility.
- Others combine zebra crossings with pedestrian traffic signals.

Despite these differences, the purpose remains the same: **to provide a safe place for pedestrians to cross the road.**

---

## Conclusion

A zebra crossing is a simple yet highly effective road safety feature. It protects pedestrians, reminds drivers to be careful, and helps maintain orderly traffic. Every road user has a responsibility to follow the rules at zebra crossings. Respecting these crossings can prevent accidents, save lives, and create safer roads for everyone.
