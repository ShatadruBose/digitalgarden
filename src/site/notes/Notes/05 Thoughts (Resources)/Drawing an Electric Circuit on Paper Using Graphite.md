# Drawing an Electric Circuit on Paper Using Graphite

## Introduction

An electric circuit is a closed path through which electric current can flow. Usually, circuits are made using wires, batteries, switches, and other electrical components. However, **graphite**, the material found in pencil leads, can also conduct electricity. This makes it possible to draw a simple working electric circuit directly on paper using a pencil.

This experiment demonstrates that graphite is an electrical conductor and that a sufficiently thick graphite line can act as a conducting path.

## Aim

To draw a simple electric circuit on paper using a graphite pencil and demonstrate that graphite can conduct electricity.

## Materials Required

- A sheet of paper
- A soft graphite pencil, preferably 2B–8B
- A small battery
- A small LED
- Connecting wires
- Two crocodile clips or pieces of conductive tape
- A switch, if available

## Scientific Principle

Graphite is an allotrope of carbon. Unlike diamond, graphite has a structure in which some electrons are able to move through the material.

Therefore, graphite can conduct electric current.

When a thick graphite line is drawn between two points, the line can provide a path for electric current. However, graphite has **much higher electrical resistance than copper wire**. Consequently, a thin or short pencil line may conduct poorly, while a thick, dark, and continuous line generally conducts better.

The resistance of the graphite path depends on factors such as:

- Length of the graphite line
- Thickness of the graphite layer
- Type and hardness of the pencil
- Pressure used while drawing
- Quality and condition of the paper
- Contact between the wires and graphite

## Circuit Diagram

A simple circuit can be represented as:

```text
       Graphite line
   ┌───────────────────┐
   │                   │
 (+) Battery          LED
   │                   │
   └───────────────────┘
```

The graphite drawing forms part of the conducting path between the battery and the LED.

## Procedure

1. Take a clean sheet of paper.
2. Draw a thick, continuous graphite line using a soft pencil.
3. Make the line several layers thick by repeatedly shading over the same path.
4. Leave two clear contact points at the ends of the graphite track.
5. Connect one terminal of the battery to one end of the graphite track.
6. Connect the other end of the graphite track to the LED.
7. Connect the other terminal of the LED back to the battery.
8. Close the circuit.
9. Observe whether the LED lights.
10. If necessary, make the graphite track thicker or improve the electrical contacts.

## Observation

When the circuit is properly connected, a small amount of electric current can pass through the graphite track.

The LED may glow, although it can be much dimmer than when connected using ordinary copper wire. This is because graphite has considerably greater resistance than copper.

A thicker and more heavily shaded graphite track generally provides a better conducting path than a thin pencil line.

## Why Does the Circuit Work?

The circuit works because graphite contains electrons that can move through its layered structure.

In graphite, each carbon atom is bonded to three other carbon atoms. The remaining electron contributes to a system of electrons that can move through the structure.

When the battery provides a potential difference, these mobile charge carriers can move, producing an electric current.

Thus, the graphite line drawn on the paper is not merely a drawing—it can function as a **resistive electrical conductor**.

## Why Does the LED Glow Dimly?

Graphite is a conductor, but it is not nearly as conductive as metals such as copper.

A graphite pencil line has relatively high resistance. Therefore, the current flowing through the circuit may be small.

The relationship between voltage, current, and resistance is described by Ohm's law:

\[
V = IR
\]

where:

- \(V\) = potential difference
- \(I\) = electric current
- \(R\) = resistance

If the resistance of the graphite track is high, the current \(I\) will be lower for the same battery voltage.

## Effect of Pencil Type

Different pencils contain different proportions of graphite and clay.

Generally:

- **Hard pencils (H, 2H, etc.)** contain more clay and less graphite.
- **Soft pencils (B, 2B, 4B, 6B, etc.)** contain more graphite.

A softer pencil usually produces a darker graphite track and is generally more suitable for making a conductive path.

## Applications

The same basic principle is used in educational experiments and is related to technologies such as:

- Graphite-based resistors
- Pencil-drawn electronic circuits
- Conductive sketches and prototypes
- Resistive sensors
- Experimental paper electronics
- Graphite electrodes

## Precautions

- Do not connect the graphite circuit directly to a household electrical outlet.
- Use only a small battery or another safe low-voltage source.
- Do not short-circuit the battery.
- Ensure that the LED is connected with suitable polarity and, where necessary, current limiting.
- Make the graphite track thick and continuous.
- Ensure that the connecting wires make good electrical contact with the graphite.

## Conclusion

Graphite is capable of conducting electricity because its atomic structure allows some electrons to move through the material. Therefore, a thick graphite line drawn with a pencil can act as part of an electrical circuit.

The experiment demonstrates an important difference between **conductivity and resistance**: graphite conducts electricity, but it has much greater resistance than metals such as copper. This is why a graphite circuit may work while producing only a small current or a dim LED.

**Therefore, a circuit drawn with a graphite pencil on paper can actually function as an electrical circuit, provided that the graphite path and electrical contacts are sufficiently conductive.**
