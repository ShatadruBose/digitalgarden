# Polysupremacy
*A proposed term describing a phenomenon where a person's secondary, tertiary, or even later-acquired skill exceeds another person's primary or native skill in the same domain.*

**Author:** Proposed Concept  
**Status:** Neologism (Newly Coined Word)

---

# Abstract

Human ability is often assumed to correlate with experience, age, or order of acquisition. For example, many people believe that a person's native language will always be superior to the same language learned later by someone else, or that a professional musician will naturally outperform an amateur.

Reality frequently contradicts this assumption.

Many individuals develop extraordinary mastery in skills that are not their primary discipline. A person's fifth language may become stronger than another individual's native language. A software engineer may possess greater mathematical ability than some professional mathematicians. A hobbyist photographer may produce work exceeding that of full-time photographers.

This paper proposes the term **Polysupremacy** to describe this phenomenon.

---

# Etymology

**Poly**
- Greek
- Meaning: many, multiple

**Supremacy**
- Latin origin
- Meaning: superiority, highest rank, exceeding others

Combined:

> **Polysupremacy**
>
> The superiority of a secondary or later-developed skill over another person's primary equivalent skill.

---

# Definition

**Polysupremacy (noun)**

> The condition in which an individual demonstrates greater proficiency in a skill that is not their primary specialization, first language, original discipline, or principal occupation than another individual demonstrates in that same skill despite it being their primary one.

# Why This Concept Exists

Skill acquisition is not determined solely by

- Birth
- Native environment
- First exposure
- Formal occupation
- Years spent

Instead, skill depends upon

- Deliberate practice
- Motivation
- Feedback
- Study quality
- Consistency
- Passion
- Cognitive ability
- Opportunity

Therefore, later-acquired skills may surpass primary ones.

# Conclusion

**Polysupremacy** describes a recognizable phenomenon in which an individual's secondary, later-acquired, or non-primary skill surpasses another individual's primary equivalent skill. It emphasizes that mastery is determined not by origin, native status, or profession, but by the depth and effectiveness of learning, practice, and continuous improvement.

Although newly proposed, the concept provides a concise way to discuss a pattern observed across languages, science, technology, sports, music, and many other fields, highlighting that excellence is earned rather than predetermined.
